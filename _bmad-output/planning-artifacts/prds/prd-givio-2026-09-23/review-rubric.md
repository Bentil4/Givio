# PRD Quality Review — Givio Organizer Multi-Tenancy & Role Hierarchy

## Overall verdict

This is a disciplined, unusually self-aware brownfield PRD: its thesis (per-event atomic access + family dignity, not generic privacy) genuinely drives feature and metric choices rather than decorating them, its assumptions/open-questions/non-goals are surfaced honestly instead of smoothed over, and every brownfield citation checked against the actual repo (AD-1, AD-2, AD-5, AD-9, AD-10 in `ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md`; FR-SEC-002/003, FR-RPT-003, NFR-USE-003, and the §4.2 Admin quote in `docs/DMS_Product_Requirements_Document.md`; the "substantially built and in review" characterization against `sprint-status.yaml`) turned out accurate. What's most at risk is downstream usability: 9 of 22 FRs — including the foundational FR-1 — are never cited by any UJ's "Realizes" list despite their parent sections claiming UJ coverage, one UJ has no named protagonist, and two FRs (FR-5, FR-21) promise outcomes their own stated consequences don't make fully testable. None of this is fatal, but a chain-top PRD staking downstream Architecture/Epics work on clean source-extraction should close the FR↔UJ linking gap before handoff.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Trade-offs are named with what's given up, not just what's chosen: §9's Cost guardrail states manual verification (FR-8) is an ops-time cost "acceptable at current expected Organizer signup volume; revisit if volume grows enough to make phone-verification-per-Organizer a bottleneck" — an honest admission the v1 approach doesn't scale indefinitely. Open Question 5 explicitly names a real tension (does "Admin never loses full system control/access," FR-19, sit in tension with FR-2's "structural" tenant isolation claim?) rather than letting FR-19 quietly override FR-2. §8's NFRs are each tied to a specific FR rather than declared generically important, avoiding the rubric's "every NFR is important" red flag.

### Findings
- **medium** Migration decision deferred without even a provisional stance (§6.2, §12, Open Question 1) — Unlike the FR-19/OQ5 tension, which gets a working `[ASSUMPTION]` plus a flagged Open Question, the migration path for currently-live Admin/Operator accounts gets no provisional decision at all — only "designing that migration is Architecture's task, not enumerated as an FR here" (§6.2). Given §12 calls this a "hard prerequisite for shipping any of FR-1–22 to production," a decision-maker reading this PRD alone cannot tell whether the intended shape is (a) big-bang cutover, (b) parallel-run, or (c) phased-by-tenant — even directionally. *Fix:* add a one-line steer (even "Architecture should default to X unless blocked") so downstream isn't starting from zero.

## Substance over theater — strong

No persona, NFR, or Vision theater. The five personas (§2.1) are literal roles in the access hierarchy being specified, not marketing archetypes — each is required to define who FR-1 through FR-22 constrain, and each personas' JTBD maps to specific FRs via the UJs. The Vision (§1) is anchored in domain-specific stakes ("an Operator let go, an Organizer suspended under investigation," "a grieving family's own view... never goes dark") that couldn't swap into an unrelated PRD. §8's NFRs avoid boilerplate ("scalable/secure/reliable") in favor of FR-specific bounds, and where a bound is genuinely missing (performance), the PRD says so via `[ASSUMPTION: ... TBD]` rather than papering over it with an adjective — see Done-ness clarity for why that's still a gap, just not a theater one.

## Strategic coherence — strong

Two theses recur and visibly drive decisions: "the atomic unit of access is never 'this company' but always 'this specific event'" (§1) shows up as FR-1's atomicity rule, FR-2's isolation guarantee, FR-3's event-switcher, and FR-9's all-routes pending boundary — a consistent arc, not a backlog with headings. The second thesis — family dignity over generic privacy — is explicitly called out as governing "the language and UX of this feature, not just its access rules" (§4.6 description) and is carried into a Constraint (§9: "Dignity, not generic privacy"). Success Metrics validate the theses directly (SM-2: zero cross-tenant exposure; SM-5: dignity-safeguard compliance) rather than measuring activity, and both primary metrics with an incentive to game (SM-1, SM-4) carry a counter-metric (SM-C1, SM-C2).

### Findings
- **low** No internal MVP sequencing (§6.1) — "All of FR-1 through FR-22 above constitute v1 scope" with no priority tiering beyond the migration blocker (OQ1). Defensible since staging is arguably Epics' job, but a chain-top PRD could offer at least a rough sequencing hint (e.g., isolation/credentials before dignity/reporting) to steer epic-splitting. *Fix:* optional — a one-line note on suggested build order would help Epics without over-committing this document to sequencing.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

Most FRs carry genuinely testable, bounded consequences and the PRD is unusually good at avoiding vague adjectives ("gracefully," "reasonable," "user-friendly" do not appear); FR-14's "does not block Event creation outright" and FR-17's explicit either/or ("may freeze... or continue updating, but must not be blocked") are good examples of precision without false specificity. But a few FRs promise more than their consequences can verify.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-5's core claim has no post-provisioning test (§4.2) — FR-5 states the system "must prevent two people from operating under one shared Organizer login," but its only testable consequence is about account creation ("Adding a second person... creates a distinct co-Organizer account"). The FR's own `[ASSUMPTION]` admits "the brainstorm does not specify a technical detection mechanism for shared-credential use after the fact" — meaning the "prevent" claim, as written, cannot be verified once an account exists. *Fix:* either narrow FR-5 to the provisioning-time guarantee it can actually test, or explicitly scope enforcement to policy/ToS (as FR-12 partially does) rather than phrasing it as a system-level prevention guarantee.
- **high** FR-21 / §8 Performance NFR has no numeric bound (§4.7, §8) — FR-21's consequence is "within the same latency bound as v1's existing real-time Admin dashboard experience," and §8 flags "`[ASSUMPTION: exact numeric target TBD — see Open Question 4]`." Since the v1 dashboard's own latency bound isn't cited anywhere in this PRD either, an engineer has no number to build or test against — only an indirect reference to an undocumented baseline. This is exactly the kind of NFR gap the rubric asks to be unforgiving about, since Architecture will need a real figure to design the realtime fan-out. *Fix:* pull the actual v1 dashboard latency figure into §8, or set an explicit target now rather than leaving both ends of the comparison undefined.
- **low** FR-10 states only the revoke-side consequence (§4.4) — "A Super Organizer must be able to add co-Organizers and Operators, and manage or revoke either's privileges," but the single consequence bullet covers only revocation, not what "add" and "manage" verifiably require. *Fix:* add a consequence for the add/manage half (e.g., "an added co-Organizer/Operator has an active account with zero Event access until grants are created," which likely just cross-references FR-1).

## Scope honesty — strong

§5's Non-Goals section does real work — seven items, each with an explicit reason (deferred vs. excluded-as-design-constraint) rather than a generic list. `[ASSUMPTION]` tags are used precisely where an inference wasn't directly confirmed (FR-5, FR-7, FR-19, SM-1, SM-4, §8, §9), and §12's Rollout section states plainly that a migration plan is a "hard prerequisite" that this document is *not* resolving — an honest admission rather than a silent gap.

### Findings
- **low** Open-item density is high for "launch-grade" stakes, but disclosed rather than hidden — 6 Open Questions + 7 inline `[ASSUMPTION]`s (one unindexed — see Mechanical notes) + 1 `[NOTE FOR PM]` = 14 items, and the single highest-impact one (migration, OQ1) is explicitly labeled a phase-blocker rather than buried. The rubric treats high density on a green-light PRD as a blocker; here it's mitigated by the PRD openly stating it is *not* claiming to be immediately buildable (§12: "not resolved in this PRD"). Flagged for awareness, not as a defect — the honesty is the mitigating factor.

## Downstream usability — adequate

Mechanically strong (Glossary is declared binding in §0 and is mostly honored; FR/UJ/SM IDs are contiguous with no gaps or duplicates), but the FR↔UJ cross-reference layer — exactly what UX/Architecture/Epics will lean on to source-extract — has real gaps.

### Findings
- **high** 9 of 22 FRs are never cited by any UJ's "Realizes" list (§2.3, §4) — FR-1, FR-5, FR-10, FR-13, FR-14, FR-15, FR-20, FR-21, and FR-22 do not appear in any of UJ-1 through UJ-5's "Realizes FR-…" lines, even though their parent §4.x sections claim to realize those same UJs (e.g., §4.1 says "Realizes UJ-1, UJ-2" but neither UJ's own FR list includes FR-1 — the FR the section itself calls "the foundation every other feature depends on"). This breaks clean source-extraction: a UX or story-writing pass following the UJs alone would miss FR-1, FR-13 (attribution retention), FR-15 (audit visibility), and the entire Admin/reporting cluster (FR-20–22). *Fix:* either add the missing FRs to the relevant UJ's "Realizes" line, or add a UJ (or UJ step) that exercises them — FR-13/FR-15 in particular have no illustrating journey at all.
- **medium** UJ-3 has no named protagonists (§2.3) — referred to throughout only as "an applicant" and "the friend," unlike UJ-1 (Kwame), UJ-2 (Ama), and UJ-4 (Comfort), each of whom carries a name and personal context. This is the rubric's explicit "no floating UJs" check. *Fix:* name both parties (even minimally) so the journey reads as concretely as its siblings.

## Shape fit — strong

Correctly shaped for a brownfield, chain-top, multi-tenant B2B feature: UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing here (this is not a single-operator internal tool), and the PRD uses them that way. Every existing-code reference verified against the repo is accurate — `AD-1`/`AD-2`/`AD-5`/`AD-9`/`AD-10` all match `ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md` exactly (including AD-9's Function-as-sole-writer rule that §11 extends, and AD-10's accessCode description that §4.6 extends); `FR-SEC-002`, `FR-SEC-003`, `FR-RPT-003`, `NFR-USE-003`, and the verbatim §4.2 Admin quote all match `docs/DMS_Product_Requirements_Document.md`; and `sprint-status.yaml`'s own `last_updated: 2026-09-12` plus its mixed done/review/in-progress story statuses support the "substantially built and in review" characterization in §0 and §12 rather than overstating it. New and existing UJs/roles are clearly distinguished (§0's Brownfield note, Glossary's "Distinct from v1's overloaded 'Admin'" callout). The five personas nominally exceed the rubric's "more than four is a smell" threshold, but they map 1:1 to the role hierarchy being specified rather than functioning as marketing theater — appropriate for this shape, not over-formalized.

## Mechanical notes

- **Assumptions Index roundtrip gap**: FR-7's inline `[ASSUMPTION: the brainstorm's "plus other onboarding info" is not itemized further — see Open Question 2...]` (§4.3) is not indexed in §14 Assumptions Index, which lists 6 entries (FR-5, FR-19, SM-1, SM-4, §8, §9) but omits this one. Add it for a complete roundtrip.
- **Glossary drift**: §3 defines the canonical term "Access Code," but §4.6 (UJ-4, FR-16, FR-17) consistently uses "family access code" / "family-code link" instead. Same referent, different surface form — worth normalizing or adding as an explicit glossary synonym (the way "Tenant / Event Company" already is).
- **Case drift**: "Consolidated Cross-Event Total" (§3, Title Case) vs. "consolidated cross-event total" (FR-21 title, lowercase) — minor, low-risk, standard prose-vs-glossary variance.
- **ID continuity**: FR-1–22, UJ-1–5, and SM-1–6 (+SM-C1/C2) are all contiguous with no gaps or duplicates. Cross-references (e.g., "per existing AD-5," "see FR-12") all resolve to real IDs.
- **UJ protagonist naming**: UJ-1/2/4 name a specific person; UJ-3 does not (see Downstream usability finding); UJ-5's protagonist is the "Admin" role rather than an individual — defensible given Admin is framed as a platform team, but inconsistent with the human-first pattern elsewhere.
- **Required sections**: all sections implied by the template (0 through 14) are present for the agreed launch-grade, chain-top stakes.
