- (idea by user) Organizer wants: each event managed independently
- (idea by user) Organizer wants: platform handles multiple events concurrently without degradation
- (idea by user) Organizer fears: events mixing together, no separation between events
- (idea by user) Organizer fears: application can't handle multiple events/organizers running concurrently
- (idea by coach) Organizer B (rival company, same platform) wants a hard guarantee: not just that data doesn't mix, but that they'd never even discover Organizer A exists on the platform - no shared IDs, no leaked names in URLs/errors, no operator able to be added across tenants by mistake
- (idea by user) Operator should see only events they are assigned to, not the Organizer's other events
- (idea by user) Operator should be able to manage only events assigned to them
- (idea by user) When assigned to multiple concurrent events, Operator sees all assigned events and chooses which one to work first
- (idea by coach) Operator wants an event-switcher, like tabs or a workspace picker, that makes 'which event am I acting in right now' unmistakable on screen at all times, so a scoped check-in action can never accidentally fire against the wrong event
- (idea by user) Admin should manage multiple Organizer accounts/events independently without one affecting another
- (idea by user) Organizers can sign up, then Admin reviews and approves/confirms before they get platform access
- (idea by user) Before approving, Admin needs: event company name, location, company size, estimated number of platform users, company type, other onboarding info
- (idea by user) Admin fears: Organizers seeing/managing other Organizers' account info or events
- (idea by user) Admin fears: Organizers having full admin privileges
- (idea by user) Admin fears: Admin itself not retaining full system control/access
- (idea by user) Family wants live, accurate donation totals when viewing via family code
- (idea by user) On family-code entry, ask whether this is the person's personal phone; if not, auto-logout as soon as they leave the app/page
- (idea by user) Family fears: wrong family member (or non-family) gaining access to donation details
- (idea by user) Family fears: not having visibility into all donations actually entered (partial/hidden totals)
- (technique) started First Principles Thinking
- (decision by user) Bedrock facts confirmed load-bearing: (1) multiple unrelated event companies share the platform concurrently, (2) one event company runs several events concurrently, (5) family didn't choose the platform - Organizer brought them on, (6) access to an event must be auditable at any moment, (8) a person could work across more than one event company over time
- (decision by user) Bedrock facts excluded as not load-bearing: (3) recorder vs viewer being different people/places, (4) events being temporary/bounded, (7) connectivity being unreliable at the event
- (idea by user) Operator logs in with credentials the Organizer sends them (scoped to that Organizer), rather than one identity that switches between events/companies
- (idea by user) Two Organizer onboarding paths: (a) Admin creates/invites the Organizer directly and sends them credentials, (b) Organizer self-signs-up and sets their own credentials, but stays pending until Admin approves
- (decision by user) Confirmed bedrock principle: access to event/money data is always granted per-event, never at the company level - even the Organizer's own access to their events is an implicit per-event grant, not a blanket company-level permission
- (decision by user) One human always equals one Organizer account - two people at the same company need two separate Organizer accounts, not shared access under one identity
- (idea by user) The first Organizer account for a company is a 'Super Organizer' - it can add other people as Organizer too (same mechanism as adding an Operator), giving each person their own identity for audit/security traceability instead of shared credentials
- (decision by user) Hierarchy rule: added Organizers can add/manage Operators but cannot add another Organizer; only the Super Organizer can add co-Organizers and can manage/revoke privileges for both co-Organizers and Operators
- (decision by user) Revoking access never erases attribution: a person's name stays permanently attached to every donation they entered, even after they can no longer log in - revocation removes access, not history
- (decision by user) Family's read-only live-view access survives even if Admin suspends the entire Organizer account - suspension blocks the Organizer/Operator's write/login access, not the family's read-only view (which may freeze or keep updating)
- (technique) started Five Whys
- (idea by user) Why #1: Admin approves every Organizer account to manage organizer access to the platform, for future billing purposes
- (insight by user) Why #2 answer: nothing would actually go wrong if Organizers self-activated instantly for billing purposes - billing alone does not require pre-approval, which means billing is not the real root reason for the approval gate
- (idea by user) Why #3: without approval, the platform becomes a vector for scams - unvetted organizers could defraud grieving families, and the platform's credibility collapses if it's seen as enabling fraud
- (idea by user) Why #4: after-the-fact bans don't work because a scammer can simply abandon the account and create a new one for the next event, skipping out on bills/consequences each time
- (idea by user) Fraud-prevention feature: detect potential duplicate events (e.g. same event/deceased being registered more than once to double-collect donations) and notify Admin when it happens
- (idea by user) Why #1: Operators are scoped to only their assigned events due to confidentiality and data protection/privacy
- (insight by user) Root cause found: per-event Operator scoping ultimately protects the grieving family's dignity - being seen, in their grief and finances, by a stranger who has no relationship to their event - not just a generic data-privacy policy
- (technique) started Assumption Reversal
- (technique) started Question Storming
- (question by coach) How does Admin actually verify that the company name/location/size an Organizer submits at signup is true, rather than just plausible-sounding fields on a form?
- (question by user) How does Admin help an Organizer resolve issues?
- (question by coach) Is there an in-platform support channel at all, or does an Organizer's issue reach Admin some other way (email, phone) outside the app entirely?
- (question by user) Does anyone see all events' donation entries by operators across a company (a consolidated view)?
- (question by coach) If the Super Organizer sees a consolidated cross-event total, does that number need to reconcile in real time with each event's individual total, or is a daily/periodic rollup good enough?
- (question by user) Is there a way an Organizer can bypass the system without following the required criteria?
- (question by coach) Could a rejected or banned Organizer just have a friend at an already-approved company add them as a 'co-Organizer' instead, routing around the vetting gate entirely?
- (question by user) Can a family member be an Organizer for their own event, instead of going through a professional event company?
- (question by coach) If a family can self-organize, would they go through the exact same approval/vetting criteria as a professional event company, or does the platform need a lighter-weight tier for individuals?
- (question by user) Are all users' activity logged for auditing purposes?
- (question by coach) Who can actually see the audit log itself - only Admin, or can a Super Organizer view the audit trail for activity within their own company?
- (question by user) What other feature could be added to the family screen, besides donation-view-only?
- (question by coach) Would the family want to send a thank-you back to a donor, and if so, would that require the family to see donor identities they currently can't - or could it stay anonymous, like a broadcast message to everyone who gave?
- (decision by coach) Affinity clustering into 5 PRD-ready themes: (1) Tenant & Access Model, (2) Trust & Fraud Prevention, (3) Family Experience & Dignity, (4) Admin Oversight & Support, (5) Isolation Guarantee. Remaining open questions in clusters 2-4 to be resolved next: co-organizer bypass loophole, company-info verification, family-as-organizer, audit log visibility, in-platform support channel, cross-company reporting cadence
- (decision by coach) Resolved: co-Organizer bypass loophole closed by applying the same identity checks used at Organizer signup (cross-reference against banned/rejected applicants) whenever a co-Organizer is added, plus notifying Admin on every co-Organizer addition - no separate vetting system needed
- (decision by user) Resolved: company-info verification for v1 is manual - Admin reviews submitted info plus a document upload (business registration/ID) and a phone verification call before approving; automated third-party verification deferred to later
- (decision by user) Resolved: family-as-self-organizer is Won't-this-time - v1 stays scoped to the professional event-company Organizer model, self-service family accounts parked as a future direction
- (decision by user) Resolved: audit log visibility follows the tenant boundary - Admin sees the platform-wide log across all Organizers, a Super Organizer sees only their own company's audit trail (co-Organizers and Operators), never another company's
- (decision by user) Resolved: in-platform support channel for v1 is a simple logged 'Contact Admin' form, not a full ticketing system (deferred as a later Should)
- (decision by user) Resolved: Super Organizer's consolidated cross-event total is near-real-time aggregation (same consistency bar as the family's live view), with a periodic settlement/export report as a secondary accounting feature
- (insight) Synthesis - five load-bearing decisions for the PRD: (1) per-event grant is the sole atomic unit of access control across every tier, (2) credentials-per-relationship makes tenant isolation structural rather than policy-enforced, (3) the approval gate exists for fraud/trust protection not billing, driving onboarding verification + duplicate-event detection + bypass-loophole fix, (4) family dignity (not generic privacy policy) is the real reason for strict Operator scoping and should frame PRD language, (5) revocation removes access but never erases attribution - people can never be hard-deleted once they've touched money
